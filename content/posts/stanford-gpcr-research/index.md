At the Butcher Lab in Stanford University School of Medicine, I've been working on an exciting project that combines computational biology with experimental validation to identify new GPCR receptor-ligand pairs. This work has contributed to a recent publication in *Nature*.

## Key Achievements

- Developed a high-throughput computational pipeline for screening over 25,000 protein complexes using docking and interaction analysis
- Achieved 80% reduction in AlphaFold2 run time through optimization of parallel SLURM batch scripts and GPU usage on HPC clusters
- Identified two novel GPCR receptor-ligand pairs that were subsequently validated through in vivo assays
- Developed machine learning pipelines to analyze alternative splicing events in melanoma progression using single-cell RNA sequencing data

## Technical Implementation

The project involved extensive use of computational tools and techniques:

- **Structural Biology Tools**: AlphaFold2, ChimeraX, PyMOL, GROMACS
- **High-Performance Computing**: SLURM batch processing, GPU optimization
- **Data Analysis**: Python, R, specialized bioinformatics packages
- **Machine Learning**: Custom neural networks for binding prediction

## Impact and Publication

This work has contributed to advancing our understanding of GPCR signaling pathways and their role in cancer progression. The findings have been published in *Nature* (2024), highlighting the significance of our computational approach in identifying novel therapeutic targets.
